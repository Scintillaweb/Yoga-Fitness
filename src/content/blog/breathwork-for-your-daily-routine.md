Breathwork is one of the simplest tools in yoga. It needs no mat, no special clothes and no free hour. You can practice at your desk, on a train or in the minute before an important conversation.

This article shares a few gentle techniques we teach in our Breathwork Lab, along with ideas for fitting them into an ordinary day.

## Why the breath matters

The breath is one of the few body rhythms you can influence directly. Slowing it down and lengthening the exhale is a common way to help the body settle, which is why so many relaxation practices begin with the breath.

You do not need to understand the science to feel the effect. Most people notice a difference within a minute or two.

## Three techniques to try

### Extended exhale

Breathe in through the nose for a count of four, then breathe out slowly for a count of six. Keep it comfortable — if the counts feel strained, shorten them. Repeat for six to ten rounds.

### Box breathing

Inhale for four counts, hold gently for four, exhale for four and pause for four. Picture tracing the sides of a square. This steady rhythm can be helpful when your thoughts feel scattered.

### Three-part breath

Lie on your back and place one hand on your belly and one on your chest. As you inhale, let the belly rise first, then the ribs, then the upper chest. Exhale in reverse. This is a lovely way to finish a practice or to prepare for sleep.

## Fitting breathwork into your day

The best technique is the one you remember to use. A few prompts that work well:

- **Morning:** two minutes of extended exhales before checking your phone.
- **Transitions:** three slow breaths before you start the car or open your laptop.
- **Afternoon slump:** one round of box breathing instead of another coffee.
- **Evening:** three-part breath in bed with the lights off.

## A note on comfort

Breathwork should feel calming, not forced. If you feel light-headed, return to natural breathing. If you are pregnant or living with a heart or respiratory condition, speak to your doctor and let your coach know so they can suggest gentler options.

## Keep it simple

You do not need to master every technique. Choose one, practice it daily for a week and notice what changes. Small, regular moments of attention tend to add up to something meaningful.
